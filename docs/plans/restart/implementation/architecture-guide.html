Current direction

Use capable agents; keep School-OS focused

School-OS provides

  • Canonical source-linked knowledge, tasks, source indexes and coverage on Drive.
  • Explicit recipes, identity rules, completion requirements and small Python standard-library helpers.
  • Guidance for manageable source reads, verified saving and temporary-copy cleanup.
  • D5’s approved corrections, recurring actions and parent task-state behavior.

The executing agent manages

  • Its resources, practical batch sizes, task continuity and runtime recovery.
  • Following the recipe through the complete intended ingestion.
  • Checking uncertain effects using its available authorized tools.
  • Its own scheduled jobs and adapter selection, with no concurrent use of the same instance data.

No School-OS batch controller. The old 25-processing/100-listing/8 MiB application caps are withdrawn. D1’s limits on individual JSON and directory pages still apply; they do not cap a whole run.

The product’s broader principles remain intact. D2 interrupted canonical-write repair, the separate records/save framework, D7 central register/scheduler management and D8 packaged lifecycle are explicit MVP deferrals. This guide does not claim they have been delivered.

One logical run

Complete ingestion comes before the daily brief

1 · Establish scope
Read the recipe, source configuration and relevant saved coverage. The approved daily scope is all School-OS-pending emails through run start, including backlog.
↓
2 · Agent manages work
The agent follows discovery pages and processes email/reply/attachment content in batches it chooses for its resources.
↓
3 · Verify completion
Check complete relevant discovery, content processing and saved knowledge/coverage. A finished batch is not a finished daily task.
↓
4 · Compose and deliver
Only after ingestion is verified complete, create the daily brief. The agent uses its available means to validate uncertain external actions.

Fictional example: 40 pending appearances can be handled in five batches of eight, two of twenty or another suitable pattern. At 25 processed, the agent is still working. Appearance 31 might correct Friday’s deadline to Thursday.

If a real access/content/runtime blocker prevents completion, report the incomplete task. Do not substitute an ordinary partial daily brief. A separate knowledge question may still receive verified facts with an explicit limitation.

Written illustration only. No run, simulation, connector probe or test was executed. Agent continuity is a design assumption; School-OS supplies no general interrupted-write repair engine.

Updated after implementation review · 16 September 2026

Q10 decided: keep one page maximum

Q1–Q10 are settled. Operational contracts, procedures, examples and shared semantic adapters are authored but untested. The user rejected the linked-piece proposal and delegated the page-size choice.

Read the Q10 decision →

Keep one 64 KiB encoded page maximum. A whole record that cannot fit blocks with its observed encoded size; it is never truncated, segmented or field-sharded.

  1. Decided

    How are oversized values handled?

    Keep whole-record paging under the shared installed data-contract limit. If later evidence shows that ordinary processed records exceed 64 KiB or that larger pages materially improve the requested trials, change that one shared maximum to 128 or 256 KiB. Stable record IDs, locators and existing pages remain valid, so no eager migration is required.

    See the current decision →

    Current behavior: an oversized whole-record write blocks and records the exact encoded size in private failure evidence. It does not invent a second storage representation.

    Tradeoffs to measure

    Larger pages can reduce page and directory reads, but they can also transfer or expose more irrelevant material and increase whole-page rewrite cost. Connector retrieval routes differ, so Drive bytes do not determine model-visible context or billed usage. The authorized evaluation compares marked noncanonical 64, 128 and 256 KiB pages using the same already-ingested records and questions.

    Q10 does not reopen Q1–Q9, restore the generic writer or D2 recovery, or authorize broader external effects. If larger pages are not exercised, the evaluation must report 128 and 256 KiB as unexercised.

Approved Q1–Q9 decision history

The full explanations, examples and rejected alternatives remain here for continuity. They are settled decisions, not active approval requests.

Read the approved data architecture for questions 1 and 2 →

Child, family and school scope; concrete JSON; search indexes; and step-by-step retrieval and ingestion.

  1. Approved

    What exact information and links must the saved JSON records contain?

    Store entities, source emails, substantive knowledge and canonical tasks as linked records. Knowledge declares whether it applies to a child, a family, a school or a defined group. Tasks separately declare the unit that must act, so a once-per-family form stays one family task. The concrete field tables and connected JSON examples are in the approved data contract.

    See the approved architecture →

    Example: Robin’s personal math observation is child-specific; a school lunch guideline is stored once at school level; a once-per-family contact form creates one family task.

    Alternative and tradeoff

    Mostly free-text records are easier to improvise but make scope, filtering and cross-agent interpretation unreliable. Copying school facts per child creates duplicates and divergent updates. The proposed structure adds a small amount of explicit metadata, with no generic write engine.

    The exact record fields, references, dates, scope rules and task granularity are approved. Q10 keeps those whole-record shapes and blocks a record that exceeds the shared 64 KiB page maximum.

  2. Approved

    How should the index find all relevant information about a child and topic over time?

    Use paged, rebuildable indexes by applicability, topic and time, with source/month routes and explicit index coverage. Resolve the child’s dated family/school memberships, fetch matching records, keep personal observations separate from general guidance, and synthesize the timeline with sources.

    See the approved architecture →

    Example: For Robin’s math evolution, retrieve Robin’s dated math feedback. Read relevant school guidance as context; do not turn a school-wide math update or a sibling’s report into feedback about Robin.

    Alternative and tradeoff

    Scanning a whole year avoids maintaining topic indexes but grows costly. A search/vector service adds a new dependency and does not itself establish exact scope or completeness. The proposal uses bounded Drive pages and explicit aliases, with a documented fallback when an index is stale.

    The index entries, membership history, alias rules, completeness limits and end-to-end query traces are approved. Efficiency is designed for; it has not been measured.

  3. Approved

    Should a daily run cover every School-OS-pending item through the start of that run?

    Yes. Complete all relevant not-ingested emails in configured scope through run start, including backlog. Later arrivals belong to the next run. Gmail read/unread status is not School-OS processing state.

    Example and recorded limits

    Example: A run starts at 06:00. A school email opened yesterday still needs ingestion if School-OS never processed it. A 06:05 arrival belongs to the next run.

    No further approval needed. This is a finite input boundary, not a message-count limit.

  4. Approved

    How should we catch relevant mail that appears late in a search?

    Use the connector’s live mailbox access for normal daily discovery from the last completed arrival-time boundary through run start, plus unfinished windows and known not-ingested backlog. Include the prior boundary at its actual precision, follow every continuation, and use metadata association for repeated results. Do not rescan all history every day or impose a fixed seven-day overlap.

    Example: An email sent Monday but received Wednesday is found by Wednesday’s arrival-time search. An old message that becomes visible only after its older arrival window was marked complete may be missed under this simpler MVP policy.

    Alternative and tradeoff

    The rejected full-history fallback could catch more older changes but costs much more. The simpler policy accepts that newly visible older material is not automatically rediscovered. A parent can explicitly request a historical rescan. Live access alone does not establish every connector’s search behavior.

    No further approval is needed. The route must expose usable search-time meaning, timezone/boundary behavior and continuation; otherwise report the capability limit or use a supported route. Original sending Date still defines identity, not arrival time.

  5. Approved

    How precise must the original email Date be for automatic association?

    Require the original individual-message Date with known timezone and second-or-finer precision, alongside the other metadata and lookup requirements. Try a richer metadata view if necessary; unresolved evidence remains unresolved.

    Example and recorded limits

    Example: A day-only date is insufficient. An original Date such as 10 September, 08:15:23 −07:00 can qualify with the remaining evidence.

    No further approval needed. Timestamp precision does not prove uniqueness. Unknown optional metadata is not a contradiction or a reason to create a duplicate.

  6. Approved

    When a matching email appears again, what may the agent safely skip reading?

    Each logical email is fully ingested or not ingested. Full means body and all required attachments were extracted, saved and checked; partial-email processing is outside MVP. A unique supported metadata match to a fully ingested email can reuse that result and skip content unless explicit new or contradictory inventory/coverage evidence challenges it.

    Example: Yesterday’s fully ingested notice appears again through a different connector handle and is skipped. A new reply has its own original Date and is ingested separately. If new evidence exposes unprocessed required material, the email cannot be treated as fully ingested.

    Alternative and tradeoff

    Always rereading is more costly. Reusing completion is simpler but cannot detect a content change hidden behind unchanged allowed metadata. This is the residual risk of the metadata-only design, not permission to ignore a known gap.

    No further approval is needed. Different handles or unknown optional fields alone do not reopen it. No per-appearance ledger, partial-PDF workflow or content-hash identity is introduced.

  7. Approved with freshness conversation

    May a parent explicitly request a manual brief despite known processing gaps?

    Yes. First warn that knowledge may be outdated, explain available last ingestion/search coverage and latest source-email dates or unknowns, and ask whether the parent wants new mail ingested first. Follow their choice; a brief sent now keeps the limitation visible.

    Example and recorded limits

    Example: “I can verify school-email ingestion only through Monday; the newest saved trip email is dated Friday. Ingest new emails first, or send the saved information now?”

    No further approval needed. The newest source Date is not proof of search freshness. The ordinary daily complete-ingestion gate stays unchanged.

  8. Decided: no audio archive

    Should generated audio be retained on Drive until the parent deletes it?

    No. After verified delivery to the parent by authorized email or agent chat, the audio work is done. Do not keep a canonical audio archive on Drive; discard accessible temporary processing copies.

    Example and recorded limits

    Example: The parent receives the audio attached to the email or as a chat artifact. School-OS does not save an extra audio-history copy.

    No further approval needed. This does not promise deletion of the parent’s attachment, chat artifact or provider-managed service copies.

  9. Decided: send together; text fallback on audio failure

    When both email and audio are requested, should email go first?

    When audio is properly configured, prepare it and send it with the email. If audio fails, send the email without audio and include a clear audio-failure notice.

    Example and recorded limits

    Example: The audio service fails to generate the narration. The parent still receives the written brief, with “The audio brief could not be generated.”

    No further approval needed. Unknown send/generation outcomes still require the agent’s available verification; no blind duplicate send or new background retry system.

D1 storage, D3 helpers/shared adapters, D5 knowledge/task meanings and Q1–Q10 stay in force. The generic record/save and interrupted-write repair framework, central jobs/scheduler register and packaged lifecycle remain outside MVP. Retained operational material is authored and untested; no current architecture question remains.
